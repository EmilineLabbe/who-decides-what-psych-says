# Who Decides What Psychology Says?

Click [here](https://emilinelabbe.github.io/who-decides-what-psych-says/) for the article.

Public perception of typical human behavior is attributed to what psychology says. This common framing masks the debates and opinions underlying theories of human behavior stemming from individual researchers.

This analysis relies on authorship data from publications indexed by Scopus, a leading abstract and indexing database of peer-reviewed literature overseen by Elsevier, one of the world’s largest publishers. Though Elsevier is based in the Netherlands, they require that indexed work have at minimum an abstract and title in English, and may not fully capture locally indexed or non-English research traditions. Global psychology research is partially isolated within regional databases, such as China’s National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI) and Russia’s Russian Science Citation Index (RSCI). As a result of this bibliometric fragmentation, Scopus authorship patterns are best interpreted as representative of what English-speakers are engaging with.
